---

## 1. Goal

Make EngCalc's rendered output stop contradicting the values it presents, and stop
presenting engineering quantities in units no engineer would write.

The memoria de cálculo is the product. A number that is silently wrong on the page is
worse than a missing number, because the reader has no way to detect it. All three
defects share that character: each produces output that looks finished and is not.

1. a nonzero quantity is never presented as zero;
2. a shown derivation produces the result it states;
3. a quantity is shown in a unit of its own dimension.

---

## 2. Baseline constraints

- package version moves to `0.10.0`; this is a functional release;
- Python support remains 3.10–3.14;
- no new runtime dependency; Pint already provides every conversion required;
- exact-first characteristic analysis unchanged;
- deterministic numerical fallback unchanged;
- dimensional-zero semantics unchanged;
- `envelope(...)` remains sampled;
- positive structural moment remains plotted downward;
- no parser, engine, plotting, matrix or characteristic **mathematics** changes;
- the Permanent Quality Gate is not modified by this release.

The change is confined to presentation. If a diff outside `renderer.py` and its tests
becomes necessary, that is a signal to stop and re-audit the design, not to widen the
scope.

## 3. The three defects

All three reproduced against `38b28d5` before any change:

| | Source | Rendered | Correct |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P-1** HIGH | `v := 8e-05*m` | `0.00 m` | a nonzero length |
| **P-2** HIGH | `k = 2*v` then `numeric(k)` | `k = 2v = 2(0.00 m) = 0.00 m` | a derivation that produces its own result |
| **P-3** MEDIUM | a deflection `P·L³/(48·E·I_z)` | `5625.00 kN/(GPa·m)` | `5.63 mm` |

P-1 and P-2 share a root cause in `renderer._quantity_latex`, which formats with fixed
decimals over the stored unit without rescaling. P-3 does not, and the distinction is
load-bearing: the P-3 value is numerically correct, so every "never renders as zero"
and every value-agreement property passes on it. The audit demonstrated exactly that.
Three contracts, three oracles.

### 3.1 A fourth site, found while designing

`_magnitude_latex` carries the same fixed-decimal collapse as `_quantity_latex` and is
the function used for the cells of a **homogeneous quantity matrix**, where the common
unit is factored out of the matrix. P-1 therefore also exists inside matrices, on a code
path the minimized reproduction does not touch.

Measured against `38b28d5`:

```text
d := 8e-05*m
A = [d, 0; 0, 2*d]
numeric(A)
```

renders

```latex
\left[\begin{matrix}0.00 & 0.00\\0.00 & 0.00\end{matrix}\right]\,\mathrm{m}
```

for a matrix whose real cells are `8e-05 m`, `0`, `0` and `0.00016 m`. This is strictly
worse than the scalar case. Two of those zeros are genuine and two are collapsed, and
nothing on the page distinguishes them, so the reader cannot even tell that a value has
been lost. The fix must cover both functions, and this reproduction is a contract.

Recorded here rather than fixed quietly, because a defect found while designing is
exactly the kind that gets fixed without a test.

## 4. Approved policy: preferred unit by dimension

Selected from four candidates after measurement (§5).

### 4.1 The authorship rule: declared units are not overridden

A quantity reaches the renderer from one of two places, and they must not be treated
alike.

- **Declared.** The engineer wrote the unit: `q := 2.8*tonf/m`. That unit is an
  authored decision and is preserved. EngCalc does not know better than the engineer
  which unit their memoria should speak in.
- **Derived.** No unit was declared; the unit is whatever the algebra happened to
  produce, as in `kN/(GPa·m)` for a deflection. Nothing was authored, so there is
  nothing to preserve, and the preferred unit for the dimension applies.

This rule is not a refinement, it is the core of the design. Measurement showed that a
blanket preferred-unit policy breaks existing contracts principally by converting
`tonf` — a unit this product defines specifically because civil engineers in the
user's practice write it — into `kN`. Any policy that does that is wrong regardless of
how readable the result is.

### 4.2 Declared units still yield when they would lie

The authorship rule cannot be absolute, because P-1 is a **declared** quantity:
`v := 8e-05*m` is authored in metres and still renders `0.00 m`.

So: a declared unit is preserved **unless the value cannot be honestly presented in it
at the active precision**, in which case the renderer steps within that unit's family
to one where it can. Honesty outranks authorship; readability does not.

Concretely, a declared unit is abandoned only when the fixed-decimal rendering would
misrepresent the value — a nonzero quantity rendering as zero being the clear case.
`5.00 m` stays `5.00 m`. `8e-05 m` becomes `0.08 mm`.

### 4.3 Families, not single units

Measurement (§5) showed a single preferred unit per dimension cannot work, because
`[length]` serves both spans and deflections:

- `[length] → m` gives a correct span `5.00 m`, but leaves P-1 at `0.00 m` and renders
  the deflection `0.01 m`. Neither defect is fixed.
- `[length] → mm` fixes both defects but renders a 5 m span as `5000.00 mm`.

So each dimension gets an ordered **family** of admissible units, and the renderer picks
the member that puts the magnitude in a readable band. The family is restricted to units
an engineer actually writes for that dimension — for length, `{mm, m}` and not
`{mm, cm, m, km}`, because an unrestricted family renders a 5 m span as `500.00 cm`,
which is measured, not hypothetical.

Proposed families, to be confirmed by the audit against the user's practice:

| Dimension | Family |
|---|---|
| length | mm, m |
| force | N, kN, MN |
| moment | kN·m |
| stress / pressure | MPa, GPa |
| force per length | kN/m |
| area | cm², m² |
| second moment of area | cm⁴ |

### 4.4 The band rule

Within a family, prefer the member whose magnitude falls in `[1, 1000)`. If none does,
take the member whose magnitude is closest to that band on a logarithmic scale. A family
with one member is a fixed preferred unit and the rule degenerates correctly.

## 5. What the measurements showed

Every row measured against the current tree, not estimated. The first two rows are
rejected candidates; they are recorded because the design's shape follows from their
failure.

| Policy | `v := 8e-05*m` | `L := 5*m` | deflection |
|---|---|---|---|
| current behaviour | `0.00 m` ✗ | `5.00 m` ✓ | `5625.00 kN/(GPa·m)` ✗ |
| single unit, `[length] → m` | `0.00 m` ✗ | `5.00 m` ✓ | `0.01 m` ✗ |
| single unit, `[length] → mm` | `0.08 mm` ✓ | `5000.00 mm` ✗ | `5.63 mm` ✓ |
| unrestricted family + band | `0.08 mm` ✓ | `500.00 cm` ✗ | `5.63 mm` ✓ |
| **restricted family + band** | `0.08 mm` ✓ | `5.00 m` ✓ | `5.63 mm` ✓ |

Blast radius of a **blanket** application of the chosen policy, with no authorship rule:

| Measurement | Result |
|---|---|
| the five files that assert on rendered units | **16 of 37 fail** |
| full suite | **25 previously-passing tests fail** (1047 passed, 25 failed, from a 1067/5 baseline) |
| length family alone, force and stress left untouched | **1 fails** |

The last row is the one that matters. Restricting conversion to lengths — which is all
P-1 and P-3 need — costs a single test, and that one is this release's own GREEN
contract failing on a rounding artifact, not a product contract. Applying the policy to
force, stress and moment as well costs 24 more.

Inspecting those 24 is what produced §4.1: they are dominated by `tonf` being converted
to `kN`. The measurement therefore does not merely size the migration, it argues for the
design. With the authorship rule in place the expected migration is close to the third
row, and §8 governs whatever remains.

### 5.1 A measurement error worth recording

The first blast-radius run reported **0 failures** in those five files and was wrong.
Pint reduces dimensionality to base dimensions, so a table keyed on `"[force]"` or
`"[force]/[length]**2"` never matches anything; the probe was silently converting only
lengths, whose key `"[length]"` happens to be a base dimension. A green measurement and
a working one looked identical.

This is the same failure mode as the false GREEN found during the Quality Gate's
historical sensitivity work, and as QG-3. It is recorded here because the implementation
will need the same keys, and because a design that had trusted that first number would
have concluded the policy was free.

## 6. Known hazards

**Moment and energy are dimensionally identical.** `[mass]·[length]²/[time]²` covers
both a bending moment and an energy; measured, `3 J` renders as `0.003 kN·m` under a
dimension-keyed table. EngCalc exposes no energy surface today, so the collision is
**accepted and documented** rather than resolved. It must not be resolved by guessing
from magnitude. If energy is ever introduced, this table is a blocking prerequisite.

**Dimensionality keys must be Pint's base-dimension strings**, per §5.1, or compared as
dimensionality objects rather than strings. The latter is preferred: it cannot be
silently wrong.

**Matrices factor out a common unit.** `_quantity_matrix_latex` chooses the first
physical cell's unit and prints it once outside the matrix. The policy must be applied
to the *matrix*, choosing one preferred unit for all cells, and never per cell —
otherwise cells stop being comparable, which is the whole reason the unit is factored
out. Per §3.1 the cell path also carries the P-1 collapse.

**Tables have the same requirement** wherever a column shares a unit.

## 7. Acceptance contracts

`tests/test_engineering_presentation.py`: **six failing, one passing** against a clean
`38b28d5` tree. They are policy-independent on purpose — they require a length to be
shown in a single unit of length, not which one — so they constrain the fix without
pre-approving this design.

This design adds three that are policy-**dependent** and must be written before
implementation:

1. a declared unit survives rendering when the value is honestly presentable in it
   (`q := 2.8*tonf/m` renders in `tonf/m`);
2. a declared unit is abandoned only to avoid misrepresentation, and then only within
   its family;
3. a homogeneous quantity matrix renders every cell in one preferred unit, and no cell
   collapses to zero.

The GREEN contract — that P-3 is invisible to the P-1 and P-2 oracles — must stay green.
It exists to stop a later reader folding three defects into one property.

Its first draft would not have survived: written at `rel=1e-6`, it passed only while the
deflection rendered as an exact `5625.00` and failed the moment the fix made it render a
rounded `5.63 mm`. A contract that breaks when the product is fixed is worse than no
contract, because the natural response is to weaken it. It now compares at display
precision. This was found by measurement, not by review, which is worth noting: the
prototype runs in §5 were built to size a migration and instead caught a bad test and a
false measurement.

---

## 8. Migration of existing tests

Every existing assertion that changes must be re-judged individually, and the diff must
state for each whether the **old** expectation was wrong or merely differently
formatted. A test that changes because the product now renders `0.08 mm` instead of
`0.00 m` is a defect being fixed. A test that changes because a unit was silently
converted away from what the engineer declared is the fix being wrong.

Bulk-updating expected strings until the suite is green is explicitly prohibited. It
would convert this release from a correction into a re-baselining, and it would erase
the only signal distinguishing the two.

---

## 9. Non-goals

- no change to stored values, only to how they are shown;
- no significant-figures mode, no engineering-notation mode; `RenderSettings.precision`
  keeps its current meaning;
- no user-facing API to override the families in this release;
- no change to `zero_tolerance` semantics: a value below tolerance is genuinely zero and
  must still render as zero;
- no plotting or axis-label changes;
- P-1/P-2/P-3 only. Other deferred presentation items stay deferred.

## 10. Questions for the auditor

1. Is the declared/derived distinction actually available at every call site of
   `_quantity_latex` and `_magnitude_latex`, or does the design assume information the
   renderer does not have?
2. Are the unit families right for the user's practice, in particular `cm⁴` for second
   moment of area and the absence of `cm` from the length family?
3. Does the band rule produce a stable unit across the rows of a real memoria, or can
   two adjacent lines of the same table land in different members of a family?
4. Is `zero_tolerance` still coherent once values below it render as zero while values
   just above it change unit to avoid rendering as zero? What happens at the boundary?
5. Does the matrix common-unit path stay correct, and is §3.1 the only additional site?
6. Is anything in §6 resolved by guessing rather than by contract?
7. Does the migration in §8 hide a behaviour change inside a formatting change anywhere?
8. Do any of the seven contracts pass for the wrong reason, or fail once the fix lands?
   One draft already did, per §7. The two written against agreement rather than against
   the absence of a zero deserve the closest reading.
9. §5 measured a blanket policy. Is the 24-test difference between the blanket and
   length-only rows really attributable to the authorship rule, or does some of it
   survive the design as proposed?
