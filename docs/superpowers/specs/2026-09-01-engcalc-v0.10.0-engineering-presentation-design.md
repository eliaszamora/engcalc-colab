**DRAFT, complete. Self-audited; four findings, all resolved, one of them twice.
Implementation authorised by the user without independent review.**

The audit in §9.1 was performed by the author of this design at the user's explicit

The working rule that follows, and that the implementation is held to: **nothing in this

## 1. Goal

Make EngCalc's rendered output stop contradicting the values it presents, and stop
presenting engineering quantities in units no engineer would write.

The memoria de cálculo is the product. A number that is silently wrong on the page is
worse than a missing number, because the reader has no way to detect it. All three
defects share that character: each produces output that looks finished and is not.

1. a quantity above `zero_tolerance` is never presented as zero;
2. a shown derivation produces the result it states;
3. a quantity is shown in a unit of its own dimension.

The qualification in the first goal is load-bearing and was missing from this
document's first draft, which asserted it of *any* nonzero quantity and so
contradicted §9's commitment to leave `zero_tolerance` alone. `zero_tolerance` exists
precisely to present sufficiently small nonzero values as zero. Finding A-3.

## 3. The three defects

All three reproduced against `38b28d5` before any change:

| | Source | Rendered | Correct |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P-1** HIGH | `v := 8e-05*m` | `0.00 m` | a nonzero length |
| **P-2** HIGH | `k = 2*v` then `numeric(k)` | `k = 2v = 2(0.00 m) = 0.00 m` | a derivation that produces its own result |
| **P-3** MEDIUM | a deflection `P·L³/(48·E·I_z)` | `5625.00 kN/(GPa·m)` | `5.63 mm` |

P-1 and P-2 share a root cause in `renderer._quantity_latex`, which formats with fixed
decimals over the stored unit without rescaling. P-3 does not, and the distinction is
load-bearing: the P-3 value is numerically correct, so every "never renders as zero"
and every value-agreement property passes on it. The audit demonstrated exactly that.
Three contracts, three oracles.

### 3.1 Two further sites, found while designing and auditing

`_magnitude_latex` carries the same fixed-decimal collapse as `_quantity_latex` and is
the function used for the cells of a **homogeneous quantity matrix**, where the common
unit is factored out of the matrix. P-1 therefore also exists inside matrices, on a code
path the minimized reproduction does not touch.

Measured against `38b28d5`:

```text
d := 8e-05*m
A = [d, 0; 0, 2*d]
numeric(A)
```

renders

```latex
\left[\begin{matrix}0.00 & 0.00\\0.00 & 0.00\end{matrix}\right]\,\mathrm{m}
```

for a matrix whose real cells are `8e-05 m`, `0`, `0` and `0.00016 m`. This is strictly
worse than the scalar case. Two of those zeros are genuine and two are collapsed, and
nothing on the page distinguishes them, so the reader cannot even tell that a value has
been lost. The fix must cover both functions, and this reproduction is a contract.

Recorded here rather than fixed quietly, because a defect found while designing is
exactly the kind that gets fixed without a test.

**And a third site: `_table_magnitude`.** This paragraph originally said the matrix was
*the* additional site, singular. That was wrong, and the audit found it by enumerating
every fixed-decimal format in the module instead of trusting the sentence. `render_table`
formats its cells through `_table_magnitude` (renderer.py:966), which repeats the same
collapse. Measured against `38b28d5`:

```text
d(x) = x*8e-05
L := 3*m
table(d(x), x, 0, L, 4)
```

renders the whole ordinate column as `0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00`, of which one sample is
genuinely zero and three are lost.

This is the site that matters most in practice. A table of a response along a beam is the
commonest thing a memoria contains, far commoner than a rendered matrix, so P-1's real
blast radius in the user's daily work is larger than the scalar reproduction suggests.
Finding A-1.

There are therefore **three** collapse sites — `_quantity_latex`, `_magnitude_latex`,
`_table_magnitude` — and the fix must cover all three. A fourth fixed-decimal format at
renderer.py:306 is a literal zero for an empty polynomial and is **not** a collapse site;
it is listed here so the enumeration is closed and a later reader does not have to redo
it.

## 4. Approved policy: preferred unit by dimension

Selected from four candidates after measurement (§5).

### 4.1 The authorship rule: declared units are not overridden

A quantity reaches the renderer from one of two places, and they must not be treated
alike.

- **Declared.** The engineer wrote the unit: `q := 2.8*tonf/m`. That unit is an
  authored decision and is preserved. EngCalc does not know better than the engineer
  which unit their memoria should speak in.
- **Derived.** No unit was declared; the unit is whatever the algebra happened to
  produce, as in `kN/(GPa·m)` for a deflection. Nothing was authored, so there is
  nothing to preserve, and the preferred unit for the dimension applies.

This rule is not a refinement, it is the core of the design. Measurement showed that a
blanket preferred-unit policy breaks existing contracts principally by converting
`tonf` — a unit this product defines specifically because civil engineers in the
user's practice write it — into `kN`. Any policy that does that is wrong regardless of
how readable the result is.

### 4.2 How the renderer knows: provenance per result

**An earlier version of this section was wrong and is corrected here.** It claimed
authorship is a property of the *unit* — that a unit is authored if the engineer typed it
anywhere in the session, which `NumericContext` already knows — and that this made the
call-site problem disappear. It does not work, and the counterexample is one of this
design's own cases. Measured:

```text
f = L/300
t := 0.5*m
L := 5*m
numeric(f)
```

`t` and `f` both carry the unit `meter`, and `meter` is authored, by `L`. Per-unit
authorship gives the two the same answer while §4.3 requires opposite ones: `t` keeps
metres, `f` must become `16.67 mm`. Authorship cannot be inferred from the unit, and no
structural test on the unit separates them either, because the units are identical.

The distinction is a property of the **result**, which is where A-4 originally placed it:

- **declared** — a `NumericAssignmentResult`, or a value substituted into a derivation by
  the substitution printer, which looks declared values up by name and therefore knows
  what they are;
- **derived** — everything else.

The call sites that were neither inherit the provenance of the result being rendered.
A characteristic result is derived, so its interval bounds and Piecewise breakpoints are
derived; an interval bound inherited from `L := 6*m` then keeps metres anyway, because
§4.3 leaves it there on a tie. A table or a matrix is one aggregate with one unit, chosen
once for the whole of it, never per cell — see §6.

The cost is that provenance must be threaded from the top-level render entry points down
to `_quantity_latex` and `_magnitude_latex`, which the earlier version was trying to
avoid. That cost is real and is simply owed. It is a smaller price than a rule that gives
the wrong answer on `L/300`, and there is no third option: the two quantities are
indistinguishable by anything the renderer can see about them.

### 4.3 Declared units still yield — measured by significant figures

The authorship rule cannot be absolute, because P-1 is a **declared** quantity:
`v := 8e-05*m` is authored in metres and still renders `0.00 m`.

**Rule: keep the authored unit unless a family member retains strictly more significant
figures at the active precision. Ties go to what the engineer wrote.** Where no unit is
authored, take the family member retaining the most, and break ties on the family's
canonical member.

Measured over the cases this release has to get right:

| case | authored | best in family | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| `L := 5*m` | `5.00 m`, 1 sf | `5000.00 mm`, 1 sf | tie → **m** |
| `t := 0.5*m` | `0.50 m`, 1 sf | `500.00 mm`, 1 sf | tie → **m** |
| `q := 2.8*tonf/m` | 2 sf | 2 sf | tie → **tonf/m** |
| `E := 200*GPa` | `200.00 GPa`, 1 sf | `200000.00 MPa`, 1 sf | tie → **GPa** |
| `f = L/300` | `0.02 m`, 1 sf | `16.67 mm`, 4 sf | family → **mm** |
| `v := 8e-05*m` | `0.00 m`, 0 sf | `0.08 mm`, 1 sf | family → **mm** |
| deflection (P-3) | `0.01 m`, 1 sf | `5.63 mm`, 3 sf | family → **mm** |

Two earlier drafts of this criterion were discarded, and both failures are worth keeping
on record.

The first was "abandon the unit only when it renders as zero". It under-applies:
`f = L/300` is an admissible deflection that lands in metres — a unit the engineer *did*
author — and renders `0.02 m` for a value of `16.67 mm`.

The second was a relative-error threshold. It works, but the threshold is a free
parameter that whoever implements the fix would be inventing. It is also conceptually
wrong: `0.02` is a **correct rounding** of `0.0166667`, sitting within half a unit of the
last displayed place, so every absolute-rounding criterion approves it. Correct rounding
and honest presentation are not the same thing, and the gap between them is precisely why
an engineer changes unit instead of adding decimals.

Significant figures retained is the criterion engineers already use, and stating it as a
comparison rather than a threshold removes the parameter entirely. There is nothing left
to tune.

### 4.4 Families, not single units

Measurement (§5) showed a single preferred unit per dimension cannot work, because
`[length]` serves both spans and deflections:

- `[length] → m` gives a correct span `5.00 m`, but leaves P-1 at `0.00 m` and renders
  the deflection `0.01 m`. Neither defect is fixed.
- `[length] → mm` fixes both defects but renders a 5 m span as `5000.00 mm`.

So each dimension gets an ordered **family** of admissible units, and the renderer picks
the member that puts the magnitude in a readable band. The family is restricted to units
an engineer actually writes for that dimension — for length, `{mm, m}` and not
`{mm, cm, m, km}`, because an unrestricted family renders a 5 m span as `500.00 cm`,
which is measured, not hypothetical.

Proposed families, to be confirmed by the audit against the user's practice:

| Dimension | Family |
|---|---|
| length | mm, m |
| force | N, kN, MN |
| moment | kN·m |
| stress / pressure | MPa, GPa |
| force per length | kN/m |
| area | cm², m² |
| second moment of area | cm⁴ |

### 4.5 The band rule

Within a family, prefer the member whose magnitude falls in `[1, 1000)`. If none does,
take the member whose magnitude is closest to that band on a logarithmic scale. A family
with one member is a fixed preferred unit and the rule degenerates correctly.

### 4.6 Below the family floor: scientific notation

Rescaling alone does **not** discharge P-1, which the audit established by measurement
rather than argument. The length family bottoms out at millimetres, so under a simulated
fix:

| value | rendered |
|---|---|
| `8e-05 m` | `0.08 mm` ✓ |
| `1e-6 m` | `0.00 mm` ✗ |
| `1e-9 m` | `0.00 mm` ✗ |

Everything below roughly `5e-6 m` still collapses. So there are three regions, and the
design must name all three:

1. **below `zero_tolerance`** — a genuine zero by an existing approved contract. Renders
   as zero. Unchanged by this release.
2. **above the family floor** — the band rule applies and the value is readable in a
   family member.
3. **between the two** — nonzero, owed a readable form, and unreachable by any family
   member. **Falls back to scientific notation**, in the declared unit where there is
   one and in the family's canonical member otherwise.

The product already has a convention for this and the fix reuses it rather than
inventing one: the symbolic path renders `v = 8e-05*m` as `8.0 \cdot 10^{-5} m` today.
The asymmetry is worth stating plainly — the same value survives the symbolic path and
is destroyed by the numeric one. The numeric path is not missing a feature, it is losing
information the rest of the product keeps.

Resolution of finding A-3, decided by the user.

## 5. What the measurements showed

Every row measured against the current tree, not estimated. The first two rows are
rejected candidates; they are recorded because the design's shape follows from their
failure.

| Policy | `v := 8e-05*m` | `L := 5*m` | deflection |
|---|---|---|---|
| current behaviour | `0.00 m` ✗ | `5.00 m` ✓ | `5625.00 kN/(GPa·m)` ✗ |
| single unit, `[length] → m` | `0.00 m` ✗ | `5.00 m` ✓ | `0.01 m` ✗ |
| single unit, `[length] → mm` | `0.08 mm` ✓ | `5000.00 mm` ✗ | `5.63 mm` ✓ |
| unrestricted family + band | `0.08 mm` ✓ | `500.00 cm` ✗ | `5.63 mm` ✓ |
| **restricted family + band** | `0.08 mm` ✓ | `5.00 m` ✓ | `5.63 mm` ✓ |

Blast radius of a **blanket** application of the chosen policy, with no authorship rule:

| Measurement | Result |
|---|---|
| the five files that assert on rendered units | **16 of 37 fail** |
| full suite | **25 previously-passing tests fail** (1047 passed, 25 failed, from a 1067/5 baseline) |
| length family alone, force and stress left untouched | **1 fails** |

The last row is the one that matters. Restricting conversion to lengths — which is all
P-1 and P-3 need — costs a single test, and that one is this release's own GREEN
contract failing on a rounding artifact, not a product contract. Applying the policy to
force, stress and moment as well costs 24 more.

Inspecting those 24 is what produced §4.1: they are dominated by `tonf` being converted
to `kN`. The measurement therefore does not merely size the migration, it argues for the
design. With the authorship rule in place the expected migration is close to the third
row, and §8 governs whatever remains.

### 5.1 A measurement error worth recording

The first blast-radius run reported **0 failures** in those five files and was wrong.
Pint reduces dimensionality to base dimensions, so a table keyed on `"[force]"` or
`"[force]/[length]**2"` never matches anything; the probe was silently converting only
lengths, whose key `"[length]"` happens to be a base dimension. A green measurement and
a working one looked identical.

This is the same failure mode as the false GREEN found during the Quality Gate's
historical sensitivity work, and as QG-3. It is recorded here because the implementation
will need the same keys, and because a design that had trusted that first number would
have concluded the policy was free.

## 6. Known hazards

**Moment and energy are dimensionally identical.** `[mass]·[length]²/[time]²` covers
both a bending moment and an energy; measured, `3 J` renders as `0.003 kN·m` under a
dimension-keyed table. EngCalc exposes no energy surface today, so the collision is
**accepted and documented** rather than resolved. It must not be resolved by guessing
from magnitude. If energy is ever introduced, this table is a blocking prerequisite.

**Dimensionality keys must be Pint's base-dimension strings**, per §5.1, or compared as
dimensionality objects rather than strings. The latter is preferred: it cannot be
silently wrong.

**Matrices factor out a common unit.** `_quantity_matrix_latex` chooses the first
physical cell's unit and prints it once outside the matrix. The policy must be applied
to the *matrix*, choosing one preferred unit for all cells, and never per cell —
otherwise cells stop being comparable, which is the whole reason the unit is factored
out. Per §3.1 the cell path also carries the P-1 collapse.

**Tables have the same requirement** wherever a column shares a unit.

## 7. Acceptance contracts

`tests/test_engineering_presentation.py`: **nine failing, one passing** against a clean
tree. Most are policy-independent on purpose — they require a length to be shown in a
single unit of length, not which one — so they constrain the fix without pre-approving
this design.

Three were added by the audit and each exists because something passed that should not
have:

- **the table column** (A-1), covering the third collapse site;
- **matrix cell agreement** (A-2). The matrix contract written during design only counts
  zeros, and a simulated fix that rescales each cell to millimetres while the factored-out
  unit stays metres satisfies it while multiplying the matrix by 1000. Verified in both
  directions: the zero-counter passes that fix, the agreement contract rejects it. Both
  are kept — the pair is the contract, neither half is;
- **the scientific fallback and the `zero_tolerance` boundary** (A-3), which is
  policy-dependent and encodes §4.5, including the case that must keep rendering as zero.

Two more were added with §4.3, closing A-4 in both directions:

- **over-application** (GREEN today, must stay GREEN): `q := 2.8*tonf/m`, `t := 0.5*m`,
  `L := 5*m` and `E := 200*GPa` all keep the unit the engineer wrote;
- **under-application** (RED): `f = L/300` renders `0.02 m` for a value of `16.67 mm`.

Twelve contracts: **ten RED, two GREEN**, against a clean tree.

The GREEN contract — that P-3 is invisible to the P-1 and P-2 oracles — must stay green.
It exists to stop a later reader folding three defects into one property.

Its first draft would not have survived: written at `rel=1e-6`, it passed only while the
deflection rendered as an exact `5625.00` and failed the moment the fix made it render a
rounded `5.63 mm`. A contract that breaks when the product is fixed is worse than no
contract, because the natural response is to weaken it. It now compares at display
precision. This was found by measurement, not by review, which is worth noting: the
prototype runs in §5 were built to size a migration and instead caught a bad test and a
false measurement.

## 8. Migration of existing tests

Every existing assertion that changes must be re-judged individually, and the diff must
state for each whether the **old** expectation was wrong or merely differently
formatted. A test that changes because the product now renders `0.08 mm` instead of
`0.00 m` is a defect being fixed. A test that changes because a unit was silently
converted away from what the engineer declared is the fix being wrong.

Bulk-updating expected strings until the suite is green is explicitly prohibited. It
would convert this release from a correction into a re-baselining, and it would erase
the only signal distinguishing the two.

---

## 9. Non-goals

- no change to stored values, only to how they are shown;
- no significant-figures mode, no engineering-notation mode; `RenderSettings.precision`
  keeps its current meaning;
- no user-facing API to override the families in this release;
- no change to `zero_tolerance` semantics: a value below tolerance is genuinely zero and
  must still render as zero;
- no plotting or axis-label changes;
- P-1/P-2/P-3 only. Other deferred presentation items stay deferred.

## 9.1 Audit findings

Audited against the nine questions in §10 by **executing** them rather than reasoning
about them. Recorded in full, including their effect on this document, because three of
the four are defects in the design or in the contracts rather than in the product.

**A-1 — HIGH. A third collapse site with no contract. RESOLVED.** `_table_magnitude`.
§3.1 asserted the matrix was the only additional site; the enumeration of every
fixed-decimal format in the module proved otherwise. Contract added, and it is the site
with the largest practical reach.

**A-2 — HIGH. The matrix contract accepted a fix that multiplies by 1000. RESOLVED.**
Counting zeros is not agreement. Found by simulating the fix and reading the numbers
instead of the test result. A second contract now checks each cell against the unit
factored out of the matrix, and it was verified to reject the very fix that passed the
first.

**A-3 — HIGH. The chosen policy does not discharge P-1, and §1 contradicted §9.
RESOLVED by the user.** Families have a floor; below it, values still collapse. §1 also
claimed no nonzero quantity may render as zero, which `zero_tolerance` exists to do.
Both are fixed: §1 is qualified, and §4.5 defines the three regions with scientific
notation for the middle one.

**A-4 — MEDIUM. The authorship rule assumed information the renderer does not have.
RESOLVED, and no longer blocking.** All thirteen call sites receive only a quantity and
settings, and several render values that are neither declared nor derived: characteristic
points, interval bounds, Piecewise breakpoints, table cells.

**First resolution was wrong. Corrected in §4.2.** The first attempt moved the question
off the call site by making authorship a property of the unit rather than of the
quantity. It is refuted by this design's own case: `t := 0.5*m` and `numeric(L/300)` both
carry `meter`, and `meter` is authored, so a per-unit rule gives them the same answer
where §4.3 requires opposite ones.

Correctly resolved by keeping provenance on the **result** and threading it down, which
is what A-4 said in the first place. The unclassifiable sites inherit the provenance of
the result being rendered; aggregates choose one unit for the whole aggregate. The
threading cost the first attempt existed to avoid is simply owed.

Worth recording plainly: the wrong resolution was committed, pushed and reported as
closed. It was caught while planning the implementation, by running the counterexample
instead of re-reading the rule. That is the fourth time in this release that something
passed for the wrong reason, and the first that was never executed at all — it was
reasoned, and reasoning is what failed.

Two candidate honesty criteria were measured and discarded before the one in §4.3 — the
first under-applied on `f = L/300`, the second needed a free threshold that the
implementer would have invented. Recorded there, because the second one looked right and
is the sort of thing that reaches implementation unchallenged.

A fifth, minor: an earlier draft of the A-2 contract carried `abs=1e-2`, which made it
vacuous for exactly the small values it protects — a cell showing `0.00` for a true
`8e-05 m` sat inside the tolerance. Corrected. It is listed because it is the third time
in this release that a contract passed for the wrong reason, and the pattern is now the
main thing to watch.

### What this audit does not establish

It was performed by the author of the design. A-1 to A-4 came from executing the
author's own questions, so defects that did not occur to the author when designing did
not occur to the author when auditing either, and that gap is precisely what an
independent reviewer covers. Questions 2 and 3 in §10 remain open and neither can be
answered without the user.

## 10. Questions for the auditor

1. **ANSWERED, A-4, still open.** Is the declared/derived distinction actually
   available at every call site? No: none of the thirteen carries it, and several are
   neither declared nor derived. The design must say what those receive.
2. Are the unit families right for the user's practice, in particular `cm⁴` for second
   moment of area and the absence of `cm` from the length family?
3. Does the band rule produce a stable unit across the rows of a real memoria, or can
   two adjacent lines of the same table land in different members of a family?
4. **ANSWERED, A-3, resolved.** `zero_tolerance` was not coherent as written; §1
   contradicted §9 and a whole region between the tolerance and the family floor was
   undefined. §4.5 now names three regions and the middle one falls back to scientific
   notation.
5. **ANSWERED, A-1 and A-2, resolved.** No on both counts. The matrix path is
   corrupted by any per-cell rescaling, and `_table_magnitude` was a third site.
6. Is anything in §6 resolved by guessing rather than by contract?
7. Does the migration in §8 hide a behaviour change inside a formatting change anywhere?
8. **PARTIALLY ANSWERED. Three did.** The GREEN contract at `rel=1e-6`, the matrix
   contract that only counted zeros, and the A-2 replacement's own `abs=1e-2`. All
   corrected. This is the failure mode of this release and the question stays open for
   the independent reviewer, because the author found these by simulating a fix and is
   unlikely to find the ones that survive that simulation.
9. §5 measured a blanket policy. Is the 24-test difference between the blanket and
   length-only rows really attributable to the authorship rule, or does some of it
   survive the design as proposed?
